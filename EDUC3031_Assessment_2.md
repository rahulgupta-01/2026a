## About this unit

Investigates issues of diversity, equity and inclusion in the Australian education system with a focus on students from backgrounds of cultural diversity, socio-economic disadvantage, rural and remote students, and students from non-English speaking backgrounds. Critically reviews relevant legislation and education policy frameworks for promoting access, participation and success for learners from diverse backgrounds.  Promotes understanding and efficacy in creating culturally safe learning environments, and teaching, learning, and assessment strategies that promote high expectations for all learners.

## Unit learning outcomes

As a graduate of Southern Cross University, you will have developed skills, values and attitudes that are essential for gaining employment and advancing lifelong learning. The University refers to these as graduate attributes.

### Learning outcomes for this unit

- critically examine factors and issues and the relationships between culture, language, identity and history influencing students’ learning experiences in Australian educational settings
- evaluate current national and state legislation and educational policies aimed at improving access, participation and success within the Australian education system students from culturally diverse backgrounds; socio-economic backgrounds; rural and remote students; and non-English speaking backgrounds and refugee circumstances, and assess their implications for the profession
- demonstrate sound knowledge of the attitudes and cultural competencies required of teachers to provide culturally safe and inclusive learning environments for learners from diverse cultural backgrounds, including the EAL/D dimension of their learning
- evaluate curriculum, teaching, learning and assessment strategies that promote success for students from diverse cultural and socio-economic backgrounds; rural and remote students; and students with EAL/D, and the pedagogical skills to successfully support students’ learning

# Rationale

**This section describes the purpose of the assessment task (the ‘why’).**

*When students in a class group have different background knowledge and level of achievement, teaching and learning can be differentiated to offer a range of experiences so that each student is able to access an engaging lesson that meets them where they are at and extends them appropriately to challenge them to maximise learning.*

# Task Description:

**This section provides a brief description of the assessment task (the ‘what’).**

*This assessment requires you to demonstrate your ability to differentiate learning for students from diverse backgrounds. You will select an existing worksheet or activity relevant to your teaching context and adapt it for two different learners, creating personalised versions that reflect their strengths, needs, and barriers to learning. Your modifications should incorporate Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles and be clearly annotated to show changes in content, process, product, and/or environment. In addition, you will write a reflective justification (approximately 600 words) for one of these learners, explaining how your strategies address barriers, promote inclusion and equity, and are informed by relevant theories and frameworks.*

**Please use the provided template file for this assessment.**

# Instructions

## Part A: Differentiated Learning Activity

### Step 1. Select a base worksheet (this must be submitted with the task)

*Choose an existing worksheet suitable for your teaching context (e.g. literacy, numeracy, HASS, science).*

*Briefly describe:*
- *Year level*
- *Learning area*
- *Learning intention*

### Step 2. Identify two diverse learners

*Choose* **two learners from the list below:**

- *A student from a low socio-economic background working below grade level*
- *An Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander high achieving student*
- *A student with English as an Additional Language or Dialect (EAL/D)*
- *A student with trauma from a refugee background*
- *A student in a rural and remote school with limited access to technology*

**For each learner, include (50-100 words per learner):**

- *A short learner profile outlining strengths, needs, and potential barriers to learning. (This is a scenario that you create)*

### Step 3. Differentiate the activity

*Adapt the original worksheet twice, once for each learner. Each version should be a personalised resource that addresses the needs outlined in the learner profiles.*

*Differentiation may include changes to:*
- **Content** *(what is being learned)*
- **Process** *(how learning occurs)*
- **Product** *(how learning is demonstrated)*
- **Environment** *(how the learning space supports engagement)*
- **UDL Principles**

**You must show changes from the original worksheet or learning activity. You must present the differentiated activities as:**

- *Annotated and labelled adjustments with differentiation shown.*

---

## Part B: Reflective Justification (Written Component). This should be written as an academic paragraph, not subheadings.

**Choose 1** *of your identified diverse learners and write a reflection of approximately 600 words addressing the following:*

- *What barriers to learning were identified for the student? (50 words)*
- *What individual student strengths did you use to support their learning? (50 words)*
- *How did your differentiation strategies respond to these barriers/strengths? (100 words)*
- *How does your approach promote inclusion, equity, and student wellbeing? (100 words)*
- *What theories, frameworks, or research informed your decisions? (150 words)*
- *What insights did you gain about your role as an inclusive educator? (150 words)*

*You may draw on:*

- *Universal Design for Learning (UDL)*
- *Differentiated Instruction*
- *Social and Emotional Learning frameworks*
- *Culturally Responsive Pedagogy*
- *Inclusive education principles*
